Working bibliography

Puritan Women and Manuscript Culture

Godly women, religious reading, diaries, spiritual autobiography, letters, commonplace books, sermon notes, devotional notebooks, household writing, scribal circulation, revision, and textual transmission in early modern Britain.

This bibliography treats Puritan as a historically contested category rather than a fixed confessional label. It follows the terminology of the scholarship cited and also includes women described as godly, Reformed, Presbyterian, or nonconformist where manuscript practice is central. The main geographical focus is Britain, c. 1558–1700; selected New England and eighteenth-century studies are included where they provide close comparative controls for sermon-note culture, religious life-writing, or manuscript transmission. Last revised 16 August 2026.
