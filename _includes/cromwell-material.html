Settler-colonial place names and civic memory

  1. 1851

    State of Connecticut. “Cromwell.” In Connecticut Towns in the Order of Their Establishment. Cromwell was incorporated in May 1851 and officially recorded as named for Oliver Cromwell.

    Connecticut State Register & Manual ↗

  2. 1851

    Donohue, Richard Franklin. “Less than Obvious: The Origin of the Name ‘Cromwell’.” Cromwell Historical Society. Reconstructs the Connecticut town’s naming context and notes local exposure to the nineteenth-century rehabilitation of Oliver Cromwell through press coverage, biography, and lectures in the 1840s.

    Cromwell Historical Society ↗

  3. 1863

    New Zealand Ministry for Culture and Heritage. “Cromwell.” NZHistory. Records that the Central Otago gold-rush settlement first known as “The Junction” was renamed Cromwell in 1863 after the seventeenth-century English leader Oliver Cromwell.

    NZHistory ↗

  4. 1902

    La Perouse Museum. “Local Place Names.” Records that Cromwell Park in Sydney was dedicated in 1902 and took its name, via Cromwell Place, from Oliver Cromwell.
