Working bibliography

Failed Experiments and Scientific Instruments

Failure and negative results; error and experimental endings; broken, repaired, calibrated, and maintained instruments; material agency; makers, technicians, workshops, and the practical conditions of experimental knowledge.

This bibliography treats “failure” broadly. It includes experiments that yielded unwanted or non-replicable results, apparatus that broke or behaved unexpectedly, materials that failed one criterion while remaining useful under another, and the practices by which experimenters diagnosed, repaired, recalibrated, salvaged, or reclassified such outcomes. It also collects major historiography on scientific instruments, precision, craft, and material practice. The final section gives optical glass and Michael Faraday as one focused case cluster rather than as the organizing centre of the bibliography. Links lead to DOI records, publisher pages, open copies, or stable discovery records. Last revised 31 August 2026.

Selection principle: this page prioritizes works useful for historical analysis of experimental failure and instrumentality rather than contemporary methodological literature on publication bias alone. The categories intentionally overlap: a broken instrument may be a failed experiment, a repair episode, a calibration problem, a labor history, and a material-epistemic event at the same time.
