Atlantic worlds, the Black Atlantic, and African political reuse: New England, Jamaica, Latin America, Garvey, Ghana, and Nigeria

  1. 1925

    Garvey, Marcus. “African Fundamentalism.” Negro World, 6 June 1925, front-page editorial. Garvey places Cromwell in a comparative canon of European and American political heroes in order to argue for a Black historical canon centred on figures such as Toussaint L’Ouverture.

    Carlyle, Thomas. Oliverio Cromwell. Translated by Rosa C. de Reijenstein, with a preliminary study by José Luis Romero. Buenos Aires: Editorial Nova, 1946. Published within Nova’s Biblioteca histórica, directed by Romero.

    Universidad Nacional de La Matanza catalogue ↗ · José Luis Romero editorial bibliography ↗

  3. 1957

    Nkrumah, Kwame. Ghana: The Autobiography of Kwame Nkrumah. London and Edinburgh: Thomas Nelson, 1957. In the preface Nkrumah identifies Cromwell among the revolutionaries whose lives and methods he studied during his student years, alongside figures including Hannibal, Napoleon, Lenin, Mazzini, Gandhi, Mussolini, and Hitler.

  4. 1988

    Hill, Christopher. O Eleito de Deus: Oliver Cromwell e a Revolução Inglesa. Translated by Carlos Eugênio Marcondes de Moura. São Paulo: Companhia das Letras, 1988. Brazilian Portuguese translation of God’s Englishman; later catalogues also record subsequent Companhia das Letras issues. The translation entered Brazilian university teaching and scholarly bibliographies on modern and religious history.
